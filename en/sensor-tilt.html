What tilt changes for radar and LiDAR

Move the sliders and watch where the bottom edge meets the ground, how far the top edge reaches, and how much of each obstacle the sensor actually takes in. Who sees what

For the chosen radar and the chosen LiDAR, at this height and this tilt: over what range of distance the obstacle is in view, and whether that is enough for the machine to stop in time at 6 km/h (decide by 6 m, stop within 3 m). Click a card to put it in the instrument above.

The numbers are the simulator's, except the LiDAR fields, which come from the repo (RES-013): the Airy 96 hangs upside down, 0° to −90°, as DA-006 plans; JT128 −4.5° to +90°; Mid-360 −7° to +52°, a random scan; JT16 ±20° in 16 rows. Radar: the return falls with the fourth power of distance and is calibrated so a man is seen out to each unit's range, the radar sim's (tractor-final RES-043 and RES-045): the maker's range on a person where one is printed, TI's range equation on a stated chirp for the TI boards, or a maker's range on a car or a pedestrian moved to a standing adult. Three figures are assumed: the SR75's cell width and range on a man, and the O-79's range on a man. Hover a radar for its figures. A still obstacle must be at least three times the threshold to stand out from the ground; a moving one the radar picks out by its speed. LiDAR: the return falls with the square of distance; an obstacle counts once at least three points land on it. Holes have sloping walls; radar gets its return from the far wall, LiDAR from points more than 15 cm below the surface.

Simplified: the beam is equally strong across the whole field (a real antenna is strongest on its axis), the ground is flat, the crop does not exist for the radar, the mast stands at the very front of the machine, and the height measurement of the ARS 548, the Hugin D1 and the DRVEGRD 169 is not modelled. The ARS 408's far beam (18° to 250 m) is not drawn.
